The CMU library holds 2,375 clips converted from the CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database (mocap.cs.cmu.edu), which was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217. “Real motion” moves come from the DeepMimic sample clips, which were built from CMU and SFU motion capture. Clips are flattened to a side view and cut to their first 20 seconds.

How to make a stickman video from text

  1. Write what happens, using your characters’ names: “Ravi walks up to Meera and says ‘Hi!’. They high five.”
  2. Press Build clip. Every sentence becomes a beat on the timeline, and you can change any move, face, timing or speech bubble.
  3. Pick a scene (park, city, classroom, beach, night) or upload your own background.
  4. Choose 16:9, 9:16 for Shorts and Reels, or 1:1, then press Record clip to download the video.

Is it really free?

Yes. No sign-up and no watermark. The animation runs in your browser, so nothing you make is uploaded.

Where do the moves come from?

Basic moves are drawn in code. Over 2,000 more are real human motion capture from the Carnegie Mellon University database, turned into stick-figure animation. You can also import your own BVH or ASF/AMC files.

Does it use AI?

No. Stickframe matches the words you write to its move library. If an action isn’t in the library, it tells you which part it skipped.
